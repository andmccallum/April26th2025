
Pi0 Habitable Zone Survey: Exoplanet Occurrence Rates and the Kepler-Gaia Catalogues
===================================================================================

Abstract:
---------
This study investigates exoplanet occurrence rates for planets with radii between 0.5 and 16 R⨁ and orbital periods between 0.78 and 400 days, orbiting FGK dwarf stars. We analyze the Kepler Q1-Q17 DR25 stellar catalogue, cross-matched with Gaia DR2 radii, and apply rigorous vetting to remove likely binaries and evolved stars. Using approximate Bayesian computation, we estimate occurrence rates, explore the photoevaporation-driven radius gap, and provide new constraints on the frequency of potentially habitable, rocky planets ("eta-Earth") around Sun-like stars. The study includes a mathematical appendix, bibliography, and footnotes.



1. Introduction and Data Pipeline
--------------------------------
We began with 197,096 Kepler stars from the Q1-Q17 DR25 catalogue (Mathur et al. 2017). Limb-darkening coefficients were calculated using Teff, logg, and [Fe/H] (Claret & Bloeman 2011). Gaia DR2 provided improved radii for 177,911 targets (Berger et al. 2018), with <10% radius precision for most stars. We cross-matched both catalogues, removed likely binaries (BIN flag = 1 or 3), and excluded evolved stars (Evol flag > 0), yielding a final sample of 116,637 FGK dwarfs.



2. Methodology: Occurrence Rate Estimation
------------------------------------------
We use approximate Bayesian computation (ABC) to estimate planet occurrence rates, accounting for:
- Detection and vetting efficiency
- Planet radius uncertainty
- Reliability against transit-like noise

Occurrence rates are computed for all FGK dwarfs and separately for F-, G-, and K-type stars, as a function of planet radius and orbital period.



3. Key Findings: Radius Gap and Eta-Earth
----------------------------------------
- The photoevaporation-driven "radius gap" between ~1.5 and 2 R⨁ is confirmed, but the bimodal distribution for P < 100 days is only present over a narrow range of periods.
- Sub-Neptunes dominate above the gap, super-Earths below.
- For planets 0.75-1.5 R⨁ in the habitable zone (0.99-1.70 AU) around G-type stars, the 84.1th percentile upper limit for eta-Earth is <0.18 planets per star.



Appendix A: Mathematical Framework
---------------------------------
A.1 Occurrence Rate Calculation
------------------------------
Let N_p be the number of detected planets, N_* the number of stars surveyed, and C the completeness correction:

f = N_p / (N_* * C)

A.2 Approximate Bayesian Computation (ABC)
------------------------------------------
Posterior: P(θ|D) ∝ P(D|θ)P(θ)

A.3 Radius Gap Modeling
----------------------
The radius gap is modeled as a deficit in the planet radius distribution between 1.5 and 2 R⨁, parameterized by a double Gaussian fit.

A.4 Eta-Earth Estimation
------------------------
η_⊕ = N_{0.75-1.5 R⨁, HZ} / (N_{G, HZ} * C_{HZ})

where HZ = habitable zone, C_{HZ} = completeness in HZ.
